# BENELUX option candidates

*Research note dated 2026-10-19. Not financial advice: a desk study from public web search results,
not a recommendation to trade.*

## Summary

Ten Euronext Amsterdam names are proposed for the owner's wheel strategy, led by Shell, ING,
ArcelorMittal, Ahold Delhaize and Philips, with Unilever, ABN AMRO, Prosus, Air France-KLM and
Aalberts completing the set and five alternates held in reserve. Every pick trades below EUR 60 per
share, so one contract ties up between 0.5% and 2.9% of the EUR 200,000 capital, and no sector has
more than two names. The evidence base is thin: liquidity rests mainly on a Euronext list of the ten
most actively traded Dutch option contracts from September 2025 and on class listings, with hard
volume or open-interest figures for only two of the ten, so the owner should confirm live quotes
and spreads with the broker before trading.

## Motivation

The owner sells cash-secured puts and, on assignment, covered calls. Both legs are entered and
exited through the option order book, so a class that cannot be traded at a fair price defeats the
strategy before any other consideration applies; option liquidity therefore comes first. The second
criterion follows from the contract size: one contract covers 100 shares, so a share price near
EUR 100 commits about 5% of capital to a single put, which is the most the owner wants in one
position; prices up to EUR 150 are tolerable with a reason. The third criterion, at most two names
per sector, limits the chance that one industry shock lands the owner with several assignments at
once. There is no dividend criterion: dividends are a by-product of assignment, not a goal, so
withholding is reported for information only.

## Definitions & notation

| Term | Rule used in this note |
|---|---|
| Criterion 1, option liquidity | A single-stock option class on Euronext Amsterdam or Euronext Brussels; weekly expiries preferred; presence in a Euronext most-traded list, tight spreads or market-maker coverage count as evidence |
| Criterion 2, price fit | Share price at or below about EUR 100; up to EUR 150 with justification; above EUR 150 excluded |
| Criterion 3, sector spread | At most two names per sector (GICS-style sector labels as recorded on the fact sheets) |
| Collateral per contract | Share price x 100 shares, in EUR; expressed as a percentage of EUR 200,000 capital. The cash actually reserved for a below-market put strike is slightly lower |
| Liquidity evidence levels | Quantified: session volume or open interest figures on the sheet. Listed: named in a Euronext most-traded list. Existence: class or weekly class pages only. Refuted: a reviewer found the liquidity claim unsupported or contradicted |
| Weeklies | Yes if a weekly expiry class is evidenced on a Euronext page or list; null if unknown |
| BENELUX | Listed on Euronext Amsterdam or Euronext Brussels, or domiciled in NL, BE or LU with a Euronext option class |
| Withholding | Statutory dividend withholding of the issuer's domicile as applied by the broker: GB 0%, NL 15%, LU 15%, BE 30%, FR 25% as recorded on the sheet (treaty relief may lower it; see pitfalls) |

## Method

The universe was built from six sweeps of the public web on 2026-10-19 and holds 96 names across
Euronext Amsterdam and Euronext Brussels. Twenty-two names were dropped before any fact sheet was
written, each for a stated reason: a share price far above EUR 150, a completed or pending takeover
or delisting, or no Euronext option class found by any sweep. The remaining names were sorted by a
quick check of their option class; 55 received that check and 21 were set aside with the evidence
recorded (low trading activity, no single-stock class, price above the guideline, or a pending
corporate action). Fifty-three fact sheets were written, one per surviving name, recording price,
domicile, sector, option classes, weekly expiries, dividend months, withholding and corporate
actions, each with its source and the date the figure refers to.

Each sheet was then challenged by three independent reviewers with different briefs: one on option
liquidity, one on price, domicile and dividend facts, and one on corporate actions and staleness.
Their verdicts are recorded as "stands" or "refuted", with issues noted. Three rankings were then
produced under different lenses (liquidity first; income and risk; diversification first), and a
synthesis resolved their disagreements by returning to the owner's three criteria in order. Figures
were validated for internal consistency and a final check looked for liquid names missing from the
universe.

Every figure in this note comes from public web search results read on 2026-10-19, not from a
market data feed. Prices are indicative last-trade quotes from Dutch and Belgian quote sites,
labelled with the date they refer to; they are not official Euronext closes. In the review stage
a search allowance ran out, so 43 of the 159 reviewer checks could not search and recorded only
what the sheet already held; a "stands" verdict from such a check means "not contradicted", not
"confirmed". The thirteen of those checks that fell on the ten picks were re-run with searches on
2026-10-19 and their findings are folded in below; the thirty on names outside the ten were not,
and those names are described from their first-round sheets only. The final check named KPN,
Vopak and BAM as never having entered the universe; KPN was given a sheet and three reviews and
appears among the alternates, the other two were not pursued. No search was run while writing
this note, and the scenario figures were stated as assumptions where no source was found.

## Scenario parameters

**Drift assumption.** A uniform 500 basis points (5% a year expected drift) is applied to every name
as the default. No per-name justification is attempted; the later calibration step re-estimates
drift from price histories. The level is a plain equity-risk-premium-plus-cash figure for EUR large
and mid caps. Dividends are modelled separately through the dividend yield, so drift is a
total-return-style placeholder, not price-only.

**Strike-interval rule.** Euronext sets strike intervals for individual equity options through its
Series Introduction Policy for Individual Equity Option Contracts (version dated 2025-08-29, linked
from the Amsterdam stock-option contract pages): each class is assigned a scale (A to F) and the
exercise-price interval depends on the price range and the scale; Euronext may halve or double
intervals in low or high volatility, and near-term series can carry finer intervals. The interval
table is embedded as an image that the search could not read and the document could not be fetched,
so the figures below apply an ASSUMED standard-scale ladder consistent with observed Amsterdam and
Brussels chains: EUR 5-10 -> 0.25; EUR 10-20 -> 0.50; EUR 20-50 -> 1.00; EUR 50-100 -> 2.00. The
only partial chain evidence is a Prosus put series at a 33.00 strike against a spot near EUR 36,
consistent with a 1.00 interval. Every increment below is an assumption to confirm on the live
chain.

**Volatility and yield assumptions.** No first-round sheet carries a realised or implied
volatility, and no search was spent on them at the owner's request. Realised volatility is a
sector-typical one-year figure (KPN's is a quoted figure); implied volatility is realised plus 200
basis points (300 for the airline). Dividend yields were verified on re-check for ING, Ahold
Delhaize, Philips, Unilever, Prosus, Aalberts and KPN; the others are approximate trailing figures.

**What the later calibration step replaces.** Drift, realised volatility and dividend yield are to
be re-estimated from price and dividend histories; implied volatility and strike increments are to
be read from the live option chains; spot prices are to be refreshed from the broker's quote at the
time of trading. Withholding rates stay as stated unless the owner confirms treaty relief.

**What the model makes of these figures.** Run as a book of ten over six half-year cycles from
2026-10-20 at a 30-delta target and a 5% cash buffer, the projection on the stated drifts opens its
first cycle with 210 Air France-KLM contracts, 4 ArcelorMittal and 1 Philips, and nothing on the
other seven names: the book serves collateral to the richest premium per euro first, and the
cheapest, most volatile name takes nearly the whole pot. Over 1,000 simulated paths the median
closing equity is EUR 206,025, the 5th percentile EUR 117,261, the 95th EUR 291,353, and the worst
drawdown 74%. Those numbers describe one airline, not ten names, and are reported here as a
warning, not a forecast.

## Edge cases & pitfalls

- Search-snippet prices: every price is a quote-site last trade captured by a search sweep, not an
  official close; ArcelorMittal's comes from an undated page, Prosus, Heineken and NN Group's from
  undated live tables labelled 16 October, and AB InBev's is an intraday reading. Differences of a
  few tens of cents from the true close are to be expected.
- Liquidity per expiry: a class that trades well in its monthly series may be thin in a given weekly
  series or at a far strike; the top-10 list orders classes, not expiries, and may describe the
  cash-settled classes launched in September 2025 rather than the physical classes a wheel uses.
- Brussels depth: no Brussels volume ranking was found; AB InBev is the only Brussels name with
  credible trading and even that has no volume figure, so any Brussels position should start small.
- Withholding reclaim: Belgian 30% and French rates are statutory; a Dutch resident can often
  reclaim down to the 15% treaty rate, and the French rate on the Air France-KLM sheet (25%) is
  itself doubtful. Dividends are also only relevant while the owner holds shares after assignment.
- Allocation by richest premium first: the model has no cap per name, so a cheap, volatile name
  absorbs the collateral of the whole book (see the scenario section). Until a per-name cap exists,
  a ten-name scenario is in effect a one-name scenario, and the airline's place in the ten should
  be read with that in mind.
- Contract size: only standard 100-share classes are modelled; the 10-share and 1-share mini classes
  that would make argenx, UCB, ASML, ASM International, Adyen or Lotus Bakeries tradable are out of
  scope.
- Earnings and ex-dividend timing: Q3 results fall in late October or early November 2026 for most
  picks (Shell, Ahold Delhaize, Unilever and AB InBev by habit, Randstad on 2026-10-21) and are
  otherwise unsourced; assignment just before an ex-dividend date changes who receives the dividend
  and the withholding.
- Concentration: all ten picks sit on Euronext Amsterdam, two are GB-domiciled and one is French,
  and three sectors hold two names each; a Dutch-market shock would hit the whole set together.
- Independence of names in the model: the scenario step treats each name's path separately; in
  practice ING and ABN AMRO, and Shell and ArcelorMittal, move together in a rate or commodity
  shock, so the model will understate the chance of simultaneous assignments.
- Low-priced names: Air France-KLM and Aegon need 10-13 contracts per 5% slot, so per-contract
  commission weighs more and the sizing floor is applied inconsistently against PostNL and Pharming.
- Missing names: the final check noted KPN, Vopak (Energy, class believed monthly) and BAM
  (Industrials) as never having entered the universe. KPN was then given a sheet and three reviews
  and is an alternate; Vopak and BAM remain unverified. Ageas was set aside on a thin third-party
  chain and may deserve a sheet.
